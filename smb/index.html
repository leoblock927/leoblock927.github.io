Project 01  /  Battery protection

Segment Monitoring Board

Power Systems BPS Engineer, Longhorn Racing Solar. May 2026 to present

8 + 8cells and thermistors monitored per board
240 mAmax passive balancing current
3isolated ground domains

What it does

The SMB is the car's cell monitoring unit. Each board reads 8 cell voltages and 8 thermistors for one 8S segment of the pack (33.6 V max) and passively balances the cells. Four of them sit in the battery box, chained in a ring back to a BQ79600 bridge on the separate BPS Leader board, which decides when the pack is unsafe and opens the contactors.

It's built around a TI BQ79616 analog front end. I own the hardware: schematic, layout, assembly, and bring-up. The firmware that talks to it is written by teammates.

Design

I drew the schematic and laid out the PCB in KiCad, then took it through many rounds of board review with teammates before ordering. I sized the balancing resistors for up to 240 mA. My first pass targeted 200 mA with 8.2 Ω parts, and when I reran the math at a full 4.2 V cell instead of 3.6 V nominal the numbers moved, so I re-specified the parts before anything was fabricated.

Every cell and bus bar sense line is RC filtered so the measurements stay clean: 0.47 µF X7R caps on the VC and CB pins, 400 Ω series resistors on the bus bar inputs, and a 10 nF, 100 V cap on the BAT pin. An MMBT5551 NPN handles the 80 V rail.

Isolation and communication

The board is split into three ground domains (AVSS, DVSS, CVSS) so communication noise stays off the cell measurements. Boards talk to each other in a ring, with transformer isolation on both daisy-chain ports and PESD5V0L2BT ESD protection on the COMLP and COMLN lines. I checked that interface line by line against TI's application note, not just the datasheet, since an isolated link is easy to get subtly wrong and hard to fix after fabrication.

I left the NFAULT pins unconnected on purpose. Fault status already travels through the daisy-chain protocol to the bridge's FAULT_SUMMARY register, so a separate fault wire would only add another thing to route and break.

Bring-up and testing

I assembled Rev A in-house so we could iterate faster. For first power-on I used a 28.8 V bench supply in place of a battery segment and probed the daisy chain with a scope. The scope capture shows the wake command from the Leader's BQ79600 reaching the SMB and waking the BQ79616, with clean communication bursts across the isolation barrier.

From there I moved to hardware-in-the-loop tests on a cell simulator before connecting real cells. At first the cell readings were off. I traced it to the bottom cell tap (V0) not being tied to AVSS, so the front end had no reference for cell 1. After that fix every tap read correctly, and the one reading still slightly high turned out to be the simulator's resistor ladder, not the board.

Then I tested the full BPS setup (SMB, Leader MCU, and HV carrier) together, since interface faults only show up in-system. When the bridge wouldn't wake, I narrowed it down to the Leader's 3.3 V rail by checking current, continuity, and voltage at each hop, and caught along the way that I had been probing the wrong 3.3 V net. As of November 2026, two SMBs are chained to the Leader and reporting, with temperatures checked against a resistor harness that mimics the thermistors.

I also fixed the repo so the shared KiCad library resolves on every teammate's machine. A Git submodule path pointed at the wrong folder, which broke the symbols for anyone cloning it fresh.

What I took from it

Bring up in layers: power first, then communication, then simulated cells, then the full system. Each layer only gets tested once the one under it is proven, so when something breaks I already know where to look.

Check references and grounds before anything else, and separate what the board is doing from what the test fixture is doing before blaming either one. Both lessons came straight out of this bring-up.
