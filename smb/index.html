Segment Monitoring Board

Power Systems BPS Engineer, Longhorn Racing Solar. May 2026 to present

8 + 8cells and thermistors monitored per board
240 mAmax passive balancing current
3isolated ground domains

The SMB is the car's cell monitoring unit. Each board reads 8 cell voltages and 8 thermistors for one 8S segment of the pack (33.6 V max) and passively balances the cells. It's built around a TI BQ79616 analog front end, and four of them sit in the battery box, chained in a ring back to a BQ79600 bridge on the separate BPS Leader board.

I drew the schematic and laid out the PCB in KiCad, then took it through many rounds of board review with teammates. I sized the balancing resistors for 240 mA and specified the passives for temperature sensing and ground isolation.

The board is split into three ground domains (AVSS, DVSS, CVSS) so communication noise stays off the cell measurements. Boards talk to each other in a ring, with transformer isolation and ESD protection on both daisy-chain ports and RC filtering on the cell and bus bar sense lines.

Once the boards came back, I brought the SMB up on the bench with the BPS Leader, using a 28.8 V supply to stand in for a battery segment. I probed the daisy chain with a scope and confirmed the wake command sent from the leader's BQ79600 reaches the SMB and wakes the BQ79616. The capture above shows a successful wake, with clean communication bursts coming through on the chain. From there I moved on to full BPS integration testing, with the SMB reporting cell voltages and thermistor temperatures back to the leader.

I assembled Rev A in-house so we could iterate faster, and ran hardware-in-the-loop tests on a cell simulator before connecting real cells. At first the cell readings were off. I traced it to the bottom cell tap (V0) not being tied to AVSS, so the front end had no reference for cell 1. After that fix every tap read correctly, and the one reading still slightly high turned out to be the simulator's resistor ladder, not the board.

I verified isoUART communication across the isolation barrier, then tested the full BPS setup (SMB, leader MCU, and HV carrier) together, since interface faults only show up in-system. When the bridge wouldn't wake, I narrowed it down to the Leader's 3.3 V rail by checking current, continuity, and voltage at each hop. As of November 2026, two SMBs are chained to the Leader and reporting, with temperatures checked against a resistor harness that mimics the thermistors.
