Why we built it

This 20S1P NiMH pack powers the car's ignition sequence, so the car doesn't start without it. It replaced an off-the-shelf pack that had logged more than 50 overtemperature events. I co-owned the project with one other engineer, from cell selection to test, and the new design cut those events to near zero.

Cells and bus bars

I compared three Sub-C 4200 mAh cells on size, weight, and price and picked the Tenergy 10505. It matches the old pack closely enough that the two can be swapped at a moment's notice.

My first bus bars were curved sheet-metal parts with split connector faces. Then I realized each bar only ever joins two cells, so I calculated resistive loss for four nickel strip sizes at 15 A, from about 0.39 W down to 0.24 W, and went with a straight 0.2 x 12 mm strip. Simpler parts, easier welding, less loss.

Protection board

The protection PCB, drawn in KiCad, has a 12 A automotive blade fuse between the pack and its output, XT30 connectors, and two 10k NTC thermistor inputs. I compared NTC, PTC, and RTD sensors on cost, range, accuracy, and response time and went with NTC probes, doubled for redundancy.

The board went through three revisions. I tried screw terminals and a sideways connector layout, reverted, then restructured the whole board to match the frame, shrank it, rounded the corners, and moved the thermistor connectors to opposite sides so they line up with the cells.

I simulated the protection circuit in LTspice first. After building, I verified trip response under load on an oscilloscope, checked readings with a multimeter, and looked for hot spots with a thermal camera.

Frame and housing

I redesigned the cell holder in SolidWorks as a 3D-printed 20-cell frame, about 98 x 148 x 51 mm, with handles, bus bar guides that make placement easy and prevent shorts, and rails that slide into the power distribution enclosure. The housing has a 0.5 mm tolerance to the frame and a cut pattern for weight savings. The pack came out 34% smaller and 23% lighter.

For assembly I spot-welded the bus bars to the cells, then folded them over for strain relief so they could be soldered to the PCB, since you can't solder directly to the cells.

How we ran it

We held weekly design reviews from the end of September through late October, kept a tracked checklist of every review item, and closed them all before the next meeting.
