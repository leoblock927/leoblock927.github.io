This 20S1P NiMH pack powers the car's ignition sequence. It replaced an off-the-shelf pack that had logged more than 50 overtemperature events. I co-owned the project with one other engineer, from cell selection to test, and the new design cut those events to near zero.

I compared three Sub-C 4200 mAh cells and picked the Tenergy 10505, so our pack stays close enough to the old one to swap in at a moment's notice. For the bus bars, I calculated resistive loss for four nickel strip sizes at 15 A and went with 0.2 x 12 mm. My first concepts were curved, split parts, and the numbers showed a straight strip did the job better.

The protection PCB, drawn in KiCad, has a 12 A automotive blade fuse and two 10k NTC thermistor inputs for redundancy, and it went through three revisions after design reviews. I simulated the protection circuit in LTspice first. After building, I verified trip response under load on an oscilloscope, checked readings with a multimeter, and looked for hot spots with a thermal camera.

I also redesigned the cell holder in SolidWorks as a 3D-printed 20-cell frame with handles, bus bar guides, and rails that slide into the power distribution enclosure. The pack came out 34% smaller and 23% lighter. I spot-welded the bus bars to the cells and folded them over for strain relief before soldering them to the PCB.
