What it is

What it is

The data acquisition harness connects 14 telemetry PCBs across 65 feet of sheathed cable on High Noon, the team's 2026 car. It carries the CAN bus and power that let the car report what every sensor is seeing, so if it fails, the team is driving blind.

Planning in CAD first

Planning in CAD first

I routed every branch through the full vehicle CAD in EPLAN, so each length came from the real car instead of a tape measure and a guess. That let me cut every wire to its final length on the bench, before the harness ever went near the chassis.

I produced 17 pages of schematics, pinouts, and manufacturing documentation. The goal was that anyone on the team could pick up a page and build or repair a branch without asking me.

Building it

Building it

I led the build with teammates who were newer to harness work and taught crimping, soldering, and heat shrink technique as we went. Everything was terminated by hand, and every branch got a label so it could be traced once it was buried in the car.

Before installation I inspected all 124 connector joints and continuity-tested every circuit. The build came in with a 2% defect rate, and every one of those was caught and fixed on the bench instead of in the car.

Because it was hand-built, the quality came from process: one crimp tool per terminal family, a pull test on every crimp, and a continuity check on every circuit before a branch was called done.

On track

On track

The harness ran at the 2026 Formula Sun Grand Prix with zero on-track failures. I also drove High Noon in all the dynamics events and track days there, so I got to see the harness from the driver's seat.

The car also taught me something. Its enclosure pinouts had no pin for CAN cable shielding, so shields had to be tied together with soldered jumpers. That is the first thing I fixed in the 2027 harness.
