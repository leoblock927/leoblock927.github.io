Choosing the car

I wanted a hands-on project where I could put what I'd learned from forums and videos to the test, and learn to drive manual along the way. As a high schooler, it had to be cheap, reliable, easy to fix, and have a big community for parts and questions. I built a design matrix scoring six 1990s cars on reliability, community, style, and power.

The Integra won. After a month of searching I found an LS with the B18B1 and a 5-speed on Facebook Marketplace three hours away in Dallas, and we trailered it home so it wouldn't overheat on the drive. I taught myself to drive manual on it.

Electrical

The car has a 30-year-old harness with very little documentation, so most electrical work starts with tracing circuits by hand before cutting anything. I wired a CarPlay head unit through an adapter harness, splicing and heat-shrinking each connection. It didn't power on at first, and after hours of checking I found the adapter's ground wasn't actually grounded. A new ground run straight to bare chassis metal fixed it, and that's where I learned to check grounds first.

For the backup camera, I ran the video lines from the rear of the car to the dash and tied the camera's switch wire to the reverse lights, so it only turns on in reverse. I also ran a USB line under the console for CarPlay and re-grounded chassis circuits.

The steering wheel is a Type-R wheel that was only sold in Japan. I found one through the network of Integra owners I'd built while sourcing parts, after nearly getting scammed by a fake seller. Its airbag used a Japanese connector, so I cut the US connector off the old airbag and spliced it onto the new wheel. It plugged in with no airbag warning.

Interior swap

I swapped the whole interior from tan to black. Removal took 30 to 40 hours over a few weekends, and reinstalling took another 40 to 50. I bought a black dash, carpet, and door panels from other owners, painted the pieces that never came in black (seatbelts and visors included), and built a suede headliner by stripping the old one, sanding it, and gluing new material over it. Moving the original wiring into the new dash meant stretching some harness runs to reach the gauge cluster.

Not everything went perfectly. The used dash had a broken A/C mounting tab and an opening cut too wide for my head unit kit, so both are wedged and taped for now. Today I'd model and 3D-print a proper bracket.

Mechanical

The car overheated on an early drive. I replaced the 30-year-old radiator, then found the new double-core unit didn't fit with the stock fans and sourced slimmer ones. A clogged A/C turned out to be a second cause. Since then I've run a compression test, replaced the spark plugs and water pump, and installed a rear strut bar, adjustable coilovers, and new front CV axles.

I paid for parts by working during the week in high school and did the work on weekends.
